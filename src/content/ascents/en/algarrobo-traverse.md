## Description
This traverse links 3 connected summits of the Aconcagua valley: Guanaco, Algarrobo and Chivato. All three summits border the Los Libertadores highway and the Cuesta de Chacabuco. Specifically, this traverse starts in the village of San Vicente at the foot of Guanaco and ends at the cross on the Cuesta.

I recommend doing it in late September/early October, since the route has a great variety of flora that makes it much more beautiful. I also recommend this time of year for the temperatures.


## The route
It starts in San Vicente at blabla, specifically blabla. From here it's about X kilometers until you finally get onto the Guanaco ridge, where it gets good.
The hardest part is this section with the white X rock. I struggled a lot to decide where to go through; I even scrambled up the side, but it was very exposed. I decided to go around the hill and then climb an endless scree slope that leaves you on the summit ridge. Although it's very tiring, it's well protected by plenty of vegetation and with two trekking poles it's quite doable. From here a few scrambles remain until you reach the real summit.

Past the summit, the rest of the traverse is technically simple and becomes just a physical challenge. On the way down from Guanaco there's a giant Quillay tree where I recommend taking a loong break; I had lunch here because it was already noon.
Coming down from the quillay you connect with a muleteers' trail and then the dirt road that leads to the Algarrobo antennas. It's a tedious but easy climb that takes you to the highest point of the traverse at 1950 m a.s.l. Although the view of the cordillera and … describe the summits… is nice, all the antennas make it a bit unpleasant, so I quickly headed down to the next summit.
I'd say this is the most beautiful part: you go along the southeast slope and there's lots of vegetation, where I came across some horses grazing. It has a few simple scrambles but nothing out of this world (references to the photos for the pretty bits).

Here you reach the big rock that looks like the summit, but it isn't!! It would definitely be really fun to climb it, but I didn't dare being alone and without any proper gear. The summit push can be done from behind the massif you see from this point (which is the actual marked route from the Cuesta) or directly up the scree. I took the scree since at this point I didn't know there was another trail. Short but tiring, you finally reach the last summit at 17, after 1700 m of elevation gain and x km. The reward is the beautiful Chilean flag with a wonderful view of the cordillera (reference) and the Cuesta de Chacabuco, where you can see the final destination at the cross. It looks far away, and it is.

Honestly, the worst part of the whole route is the descent from Chivato. The fatigue shows, and once you leave the ridge there's no wind and it starts getting very hot. Besides, the path follows a well-marked but steep and hard trail, which makes it very slippery. You have to go down carefully and work around the rocks. The fun part of this section is finally being on the rocks you can see when crossing the Cuesta, mainly from Santiago to Los Andes (reference to photos). The vegetation also makes this part worth it; there are lots of flowers (look up which ones). Unfortunately, almost all of them had recently withered. One or two weeks earlier would have been the best time.
Once that part is done, you end up right above the tunnel. For someone from the valley who travels to Santiago all the time, this moment is very special, since you're in an extremely familiar place, but from a completely new perspective.
From here, you connect with the Cuesta. If you're already very tired, you can finish here. I thought about it, but the route really ends at the Cross. It's only 2 extra km and you can go over some of the hills along the side, which are green in spring and worth it for a view of the feat you've just completed [ref to photo].
Once at the cross, you can call the summit and finally look at the (almost) complete traverse: 19 km and 1700 meters of elevation gain.


## Final (personal) comments
This route has great sentimental value for me for two reasons. The first is that these are the hills I've seen my whole life on my way to Santiago and back, and from many places in Los Andes. As always, my mom was the one who made me really notice them, and I was always struck by how pointy Chivato and Guanaco are.
The other reason is the moment in my life when I did the route: I was going through a difficult personal time and was in the final stretch of my master's thesis, which had been so hard for me. For these reasons I had stopped doing "trekking" and mountaineering, but I saw an announcement from the RAI about climbing a hill in the Cajón del Maipo that motivated me enormously. Since I hadn't done anything in so long, I set myself the challenge of doing a route alone to test myself first. Obviously I went from 0 to 100 and chose this not-at-all-easy route, for which I also couldn't find any information about someone having linked all three (someone probably has, but I found nothing after searching a lot). I thought at that moment that I had to start doing what I love and challenge myself in a positive way, and what better way to do it than with something that had been on my mind for so many years.

From the highway it's easy to picture the route; it gives a very good perspective of how the hills can be linked. I used Google Earth to estimate a route and the distances (which turned out to be quite close to reality). My problem was Chivato, since there were routes for the other two and it was obvious at first glance that this was the most difficult hill. I decided to go up from San Vicente because it seemed easiest in terms of access; however, you can start from anywhere on the slopes of Chivato, even from the highway. The problem is not crossing private land.

It makes me very happy now every time I travel or have a full view of the three summits; I always try to pick the window seat with the best view and think: that route is mine. I remember how happy and satisfied I felt when I reached the cross, and my mom came to pick me up and I told her everything.

My musical pick for this ascent is Disorder by Joy Division, mostly because of the moment and what it meant.
